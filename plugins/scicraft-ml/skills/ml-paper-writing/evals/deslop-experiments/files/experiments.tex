\section{Experiments: Putting Weft to the Test}\label{sec:exp}

It is worth noting that our evaluation is not just a leaderboard but a careful, comprehensive study of frozen representations. We evaluate on 12 downstream tasks---8 classification and 4 regression---using a linear probe on frozen embeddings, with 5 seeds per task.

\paragraph{Three pillars of evaluation.} Importantly, we compare against 14 methods: 5 pretrained tabular foundation models, 6 per-dataset models including XGBoost \citep{chen2016xgboost}, and 3 baselines. Methods are ranked by mean rank and by Elo \citep{elo1978}; paired differences are tested with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test over seeds, corrected with Benjamini--Hochberg \citep{bh1995}. Each value below is the mean over the 5 seeds, and the number after it is the standard deviation across seeds.

\weft{} is the strongest method overall: it wins on 7 of 12 tasks and reaches a mean rank of 2.4 against 5.1 for TabFM-L --- the best published model. On regression, gradient-boosted trees fit per dataset still win on 3 of 4 tasks; \weft{} sees only frozen features there, not the raw columns.
