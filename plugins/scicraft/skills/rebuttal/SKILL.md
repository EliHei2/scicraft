# rebuttal

How to answer peer review so that the reply is short, exact and checkable, and the revised paper shows reviewers
exactly what changed. Distilled from one machine-learning conference paper taken through two discussion rounds,
a revised manuscript and a note to the area chair, with a lead author and senior co-authors who rejected most
first drafts, and from one journal major revision of a computational methods paper (section 11). Every rule below is something they asked for or threw out. Where the author at hand says otherwise,
their rule wins: write it into the rules file (section 0) and follow it.

> **PRIME DIRECTIVE: answer exactly what each reviewer asked, lead with the strongest result the paper supports, and
> make every sentence checkable against the paper as posted, with its line number.** Nothing goes in that the paper
> does not print; nothing a reviewer asked for is left unanswered.

Three failures cost the most time. Guard against them from the first minute:

1. **Rebuilding the author's draft.** A three-hour rebuild by a dozen agents in an imported format was thrown out in
   one message: "the responses I made with chat were so much better ... just try to polish the edges". When the
   author has a draft, it is the base text (section 9).
2. **Revising the wrong source.** The folder that looked like the paper was an older draft: 15.6% of the submitted
   PDF's sentences occurred in it. A revision built on it would have shown reviewers a text they never reviewed, and
   its page count was three pages off (section 8).
3. **A number or quote the reader can catch.** Reviewers check replies against the PDF line by line, and one was
   expected to do it with a language model. Every number is asserted against records at build time, every quote matched verbatim, every
   line number read from the built PDF (section 7).

## 0. Before starting

Collect by reading the project. Ask only for what cannot be found, in one message:

- **The reviews, verbatim**, in `reviews.md`, each point labeled (W1, Q1 or the platform's own labels). Without them
  replies drift from what was asked; one round was called "not even relevant to comments" for this reason.
- **The submitted PDF** exactly as uploaded, and the source it was built from (section 8 proves which one).
- **The records** behind every number the paper prints: result files, tables generated by script.
- **The author's own drafts and notes**: a draft reply, a chat log, a points document. The last version is the base.
- **The venue's rules**: comment limit, Markdown and math support, revision page limit, file size limit, the
  deadline and its time zone, who reads which comment. Verify each on the venue's current pages (section 5).
- **Senior co-authors' advice**, if relayed. Weigh the idea and check each factual phrase; never paste it.

Then write `RULES_AND_FACTS.md` from `assets/rules_and_facts_template.md`: every "never", "always" and "do not" from
the author's messages, newest first, the settled decisions, the deliverables, and checked facts with the line where
the paper prints each. Every agent reads it as binding, and when an audit finding conflicts with it, the rule wins.

Work in one folder beside the paper:

```
rebuttal/
├── RULES_AND_FACTS.md   binding rules, decisions, checked facts
├── reviews.md           the reviews, verbatim
├── replies.md           every comment, one block each (assets/reply_template.md)
├── refs.json            cited passages, resolved to line numbers on every build
├── claims.json          every number the replies and new text state, with its record
├── source/              the verified submitted source, then the revision
├── checkpoints/         replies_vNN_before_<change>.md, one per change
└── deliver/             what is posted or uploaded, and nothing else
```

## 1. Triage: what goes in

Draw up, per reviewer, the list of what they asked. Map every paragraph of every reply to an item on that list or
to the paper's main claim. Anything unmapped goes. `references/triage.md` has the procedure.

- **Answer what was asked, in the reviewer's own terms.** Each labeled point gets a direct answer, part by part.
- **Add a new result only when it answers a point or clearly carries the main claim.** A side result that opens a new
  line of questions stays out of the replies. After "I told you to be minimalistic", one draft fell from 21 pages,
  45 answers and 15 tables to 9 pages, 13 answers and 8 tables.
- **Lead with strength.** Order each reply and the general response by importance; the strongest result the records
  support comes first, stated once, in the field's standard terms.
- **Make unasked corrections quietly in the paper.** Do not announce them in the reply.
- **Name the comparison set whenever a count appears.** "... methods of what?" was the author's note on a bare count
  of methods; the fix named the kinds of method in the set ("[N] compared methods ([the kinds])").
- **Say what holds for all in the title, without a count.** A figure title of the form "all N ablations lower the
  score, M of them significantly" was struck as "absolute AI slop" and drew "then they will ask aha so [the rest]
  not". The title that held states the finding in plain words ("Each component of [the model] contributes to [the
  outcome]"). The body sentence that replaced the count, "all N lower the combined score, and each significantly
  lowers at least one task", is true, and the per-ablation table with every test stays in the appendix. If a
  reviewer asks for the count, give it. The caption rule is in the sibling skill `latex-typesetting`
  (`latex-typesetting/references/tables-captions.md`).

**The integrity floor.** Choosing what to lead with is presentation; changing what the records support is
misreporting. Several requests in the source work asked to drop or dilute weak results. The forms kept here are:

- never state anything the paper does not support, and never let a juxtaposition imply a claim the paper disclaims;
- answer what a reviewer asked, including a result that is weak, stated once and plainly beside what holds;
- keep every omitted number available in the paper or its appendix, with the full tables complete;
- a result that contradicts a claim changes the claim;
- apply any reporting threshold (which p values print) to every result alike and say so in the paper; never switch
  tests to get a smaller p (see `stats-reporting`);
- keep prepared answers for known weak spots in a private `NOTES.md`, ready if the point is raised.

## 2. New experiments

Experiments run during the discussion go through an adversarial selector before anything is written about them.

- **One read-only agent argues each new result both ways** and writes a verdict per result (ADD, APPENDIX, OUT) with
  `assets/briefs/selector_brief.md`. One round admitted 3 of its candidates. The selector also picks the form: a
  table over a figure when 42 near-zero dots "read as nothing".
- **Report a pre-declared endpoint whatever it shows**, and only that step, not every look along the way.
- **Make conditions uniform** so no reviewer asks why one row ran at 10k steps and another at 30k: one training
  length per study, stated once per caption, and every condition read against an identically trained control.
- **Pair every backbone or architecture swap with a matched control**: your own model trained for the same steps,
  named `<Model>-<steps>`. Compare every arm at the latest checkpoint all arms reached ("just include the latest
  checkpoint"; earlier ones added rows and no information). Write this inclusion rule into the paper's methods and
  apply it to every method alike, whichever way the rows fall.
- **A cost or run-time column compares like with like or stays out.** One was built four times over unmatched GPUs
  and dropped ("bad column"). Before promising one, check that comparable records exist; state a confound (unmatched
  GPUs, comparators that embed only the labeled items while the method embeds every input) in one line and ask the
  author before building. Print plain decimals: one "≤" in a cell broke the decimal alignment.
- **Keep a ledger** (`WINS.md`, from `assets/WINS_template.md`): each result, where it sits in the paper and the
  replies, its record file, what was held back and why, and the slots where pending results will land.
- **When new results may have arrived**, use the last delivered PDF as the watermark and say in the first line
  whether anything new reached the machine (`references/process.md`, taking in new results).

## 3. The general response

Written for the area chair, who reads it in about 30 seconds. Posted once. `references/reply-structure.md` has the
template and a filled skeleton.

- **Half a page at most.** "General reply: 1p max ... Details are the enemy of a clear message" (the senior author).
  Final versions ran 280 to 400 words, 1,900 to 2,700 characters.
- **Shape:** thanks and the shared concern ("We agree that ..."), the two main changes as numbered bold items, three
  to six bullets of results (bold topic, one sentence each, the asking reviewer's tag), one closing line pointing to
  the replies and saying changes are highlighted in blue.
- **No tables, line numbers, method details or p values in it.** Those go in the reply to the reviewer who asked.
- **Ranks and plain heads over test statistics** in any summary: "talk about ranks, and what changed from the
  submitted version and what we added".

## 4. The reply to each reviewer

Exactly one comment per reviewer. Six comments for three reviewers drew "why do we have a total of 5 instead of 4".

- **Each point:** the reviewer's words as an italic blockquote, then **one bold sentence that states exactly what
  the cited lines show**, then one small table copied from the paper or two to three evidence sentences with line
  numbers. "Significantly above k of the n comparators", with k and n printed, replaced a bare "the gains are
  significant". When the answer is yes, the bold lead is "Yes."
- **Open with thanks for the strengths they named**, in their words. Credit them where the revision followed them
  ("Following the reviewer's suggestion, ...").
- **Full tables go to the reviewer who asked**, sorted from most to least important.
- **Point to the paper's sections, tables and lines, never to a side report**, when only the revised PDF counts.
- **Mention a running experiment only if it is running**, with when it lands.
- **Close** with thanks tied to what the requests produced and a request to reassess. To a reviewer who stays
  negative, end calmly: "We know of no point of the original review that we left unanswered."
- **Tone in public is polite.** The reply to the most critical reviewer is the most polite one: that reviewer will
  not be convinced, and the reply is there to set the record straight. Confront only with the reviewer's own
  words and leave an exit: one question they can answer by moving one step.
- **Do not repeat attack phrases and never concede a quantifier.** "ranks lower", never "is worse"; never "we never
  claimed to be best on every task"; check polite phrases for hidden admissions ("we left them for future work"
  conceded the point).
- **Voice:** full declarative sentences, one idea each; no inline colons, semicolons or dashes in our sentences; no
  slogans ("With X, Y and Z fixed, the model matters" was struck); no "we tested whether" frames; one name per thing.
  Run `humanizer` on anything you composed, then check that no claim changed: one humanizer pass turned a result
  into "results remain mixed".

## 5. Platform limits

Every limit below was observed on one platform in late 2026. **Verify each on the venue's current pages before
building**, and write the verified value and date into `RULES_AND_FACTS.md`. `references/platforms.md` has the
detail and the journal case.

| Item | Observed value | Verify |
|---|---|---|
| characters per comment | 5,000, one comment per reviewer, no "part 1/2" | count with the script; ask whether it counts bytes |
| Markdown | CommonMark with tables; raw HTML and images show literally | a figure can only be a link |
| line breaks | a single break is ignored; blank line between "Best regards," and "The authors" | the platform preview |
| math | MathJax; display `$$...$$`; color by `$\color{red}{\textsf{...}}$`, boxes do not wrap | darkgreen renders, else green |
| title field | "General response to all reviewers: main changes and new results", "Response to Reviewer X" | |
| readers | everyone; the note to the chair to "Area Chairs and Program Chairs" only | the form's options |
| revised PDF | 10 main pages, under 50 MB, an AI-use statement section required | the call for papers |
| text fields (abstract) | plain text; `*` and `**` only | |

- **Write complete first, then cut words before numbers.** Trim your own additions first, prose before quotes or
  tables. Final margins of 9 to 15 characters held.
- **Post in order**: the public reply first, the private note to the chair within minutes after it, other replies
  last. Check the form's live state before posting.
- **A promise in a reply is a deliverable.** "we will repeat this sentence in the caption ... before the end of the
  discussion period" commits the author to uploading a revised PDF in time; say so when it is written.

## 6. The note to the area chair or editor

Private, firm and brief: evidence first, then the reviewer's own words, then one request. It was rejected when soft
("just words and whiny") and accepted when "evidence-rich". The target is the author's final note in
`references/chair-note.md` (ten steps); the short form below was an earlier draft. The final note opens with thanks
and "We fully respect the reviewer's right to ask questions", places the rating against the other reviews, says
that the rating now rests on a request outside the venue's scope, and ends its evidence with the other reviewers'
own words before the one request.

The earlier short form, called "way better" at its time and then replaced by the final note:

1. The result in the first sentence, then "We write about one matter that bears on how the reviews are weighed."
2. The contradiction in one sentence: the rating against the reviewer's own written assessment.
3. The reviewer's most positive sentences as block quotes, short and exact.
4. A table: original concern, revision with lines, the reviewer's words after the rebuttal.
5. One bold paragraph of the global result with line numbers.
6. Where the reviewer's statements tell only part of the result: their phrase in red beside the full result in dark
   green, introduced as "reviewer in red, full result in green". Write "tell only part of these results", never "do
   not match", when their numbers came from your own tables.
7. The venue's criterion, quoted verbatim from the official guideline page after checking its address, and "We would
   gladly provide evidence on any concrete point".
8. One request, such as "engage with the reviewer and request reconsideration, or otherwise weigh the rating against
   the reviewer's written assessment and the venue's criteria".

Cut prose to fit, never quotes or evidence ("don't drop quotes and evidence, keep it solid as a rock"). No
adjective of ours in the title, no "outlier", "isolated", "unfair", no second request.

## 7. Numbers and quotations

Scripts are in `scripts/`; Python 3.9 or later. `check_replies.py`, `check_numbers.py`, `check_letter.py`,
`rev_passages.py`, `changelog.py`, `docx_highlight.py` (edit pairs into a co-author's Word file, only the changed
words highlighted) use the standard library; `resolve_lines.py`, `rev_pdf_check.py`, `shrink_pdf.py`, `match_source.py` need PyMuPDF
(`match_source.py` falls back to pdftotext); `preview_replies.py` needs markdown-it-py, and python-docx for `--docx`.

- **Every number is asserted against its record at build time** (`check_numbers.py --claims`). The build stops on a
  stale number; in the source work it caught ranks one place off and counts from an older method set, round
  after round.
- **Every number in a reply is printed in the paper** (`check_numbers.py --trace`), so the reviewer can find it.
- **Never replace a printed value by a recompute, and never weaken a printed win.** If a recompute differs, keep the
  print and tell the author.
- **Every quotation is verbatim** (`check_replies.py --sources`): reviews, the paper text, the guideline text.
- **Every line number is read from the built PDF** (`resolve_lines.py`), as tokens such as `[[BENCH]]` in the
  replies, re-resolved after every rebuild: an introduction rewrite shifted eight references.
- **Every absolute word survives a search of your own paper.** An absolute claim of independence was struck because
  the paper's own appendix described a procedure that contradicted it.
- **Check names of journals and venues against the bibliography**, even when the author insists.

```bash
S=<skill>/scripts
python3 $S/resolve_lines.py paper.pdf refs.json --aux paper.aux --out refs_resolved.json --fill replies.md --filled deliver/replies.md
python3 $S/check_replies.py deliver/replies.md --sources reviews.md paper.txt guideline.txt --ban <skill>/assets/banned_reply_terms.txt
python3 $S/check_numbers.py --claims claims.json --trace deliver/replies.md --paper paper.txt
python3 $S/preview_replies.py deliver/replies.md deliver/preview --fold R2=followup_R2.md --docx "replies.docx"
```

## 8. The revised paper

`references/revision.md` has every step and the traps.

- **Prove you hold the submitted source before editing.** `match_source.py submitted.pdf <candidates>` scores each
  candidate by the share of the PDF's sentences it contains. Calibrate with `--self` on a source and its own build:
  on the source work a source scored 66% against its own PDF and the older draft 23% (the original measure gave about
  70% against 15.6%). Then confirm the page count and where the references start.
- **One color for everything that differs from the submission**, behind one switch: `assets/rev_macros.tex`, RGB
  70,130,180. "not different colors bro, just light blue whatever is different from the inital submission". Round
  colors may exist in a working copy; the delivered copies have one.
- **A new float gets a fully blue caption** (`\revcap{\caption{...}\label{...}}`) and an unshaded new table a blue body;
  an existing float gets only its changed words. A label-only mark made a new float look unchanged.
- **Decide what is new by comparing with the submitted PDF**, not with the markup.
- **Quiet fixes stay black**: punctuation in submitted text, a removed box in a figure. Reword only revision text.
- **Leave untouched original paragraphs alone.** Every edit adds blue a reviewer must read.
- **The revised paper reads as the final paper**: it never mentions reviewers and holds no submitted-versus-revised
  table; before-and-after goes in the general response.
- **Apply a term change everywhere**: text, tables, figure labels, the platform abstract, `pdftotext` of the build.
- **Measure the page budget after every build** (`resolve_lines.py --pagefill`) and trim your own additions first.
- **Render every float before a reply cites it**, and look at each image. The renderer (`render_floats.py`, each
  float with its caption to PNG plus a manifest) and the LaTeX-to-Word builder for co-authors belong to the sibling
  `latex-typesetting` skill.
- **The change log is generated from the edit pairs and replayed** (`changelog.py`): find and replace pairs for
  Overleaf, old and new paragraphs for a shared document, and a byte-for-byte replay on the pre-edit snapshot.

```bash
cp <skill>/assets/rev_macros.tex source/    # or paste it into the preamble
bash <skill>/assets/build_variants.sh source/main          # main.pdf, main_upload.pdf, main_clean.pdf
python3 $S/rev_pdf_check.py source/main_upload.pdf source/main_clean.pdf --allow <paper colors as R,G,B>
python3 $S/rev_passages.py source/ --ban <skill>/assets/banned_reply_terms.txt --out REV_PASSAGES.md
python3 $S/shrink_pdf.py compress source/main_upload.pdf deliver/paper_revised.pdf --limit-mb 50
python3 $S/changelog.py edits.json --snapshot prebrush/ --edited source/ --out deliver/CHANGELOG.md
```

## 9. Polish, do not rebuild

`references/process.md` has the case in full.

- **Each round starts with a word diff** of the author's latest downloaded source against the last one you worked
  on (`git diff --no-index --word-diff=plain -U0 last/ latest/`), so their changes are kept and none is overwritten.
- **The author's last version is the base.** Read their last draft or the last turn of their chat log first.
  Allowed edits: errors, numbers, pointers, limits, and what they name. List every change in a few lines.
- **Once the author has humanized a text, its wording is frozen.** One round had replaced 37 to 90% of their
  sentences and was reverted by script.
- **Freeze what they accepted**: "do not recompute man!! we're good with tables". New points go in the prose around.
- **Checkpoint before each change** (`checkpoints/replies_vNN_before_<change>.md`) and make each edit an exact
  replacement that asserts one match, so "go back to the better version" costs a copy.
- **Work only on the deliverables named.** An unrequested comment to a third reviewer drew "why do you work on [that]
  response???". Offer an extra in one line; never build it.
- **Do not adopt an outside template** for a purpose the author did not name. A link given for a preview layout was
  turned into a red-label reply format and thrown out.
- **A bundle sent out for humanizing holds every file that carries changed text**, the appendix included, plus the
  `rev_passages.py` list. One bundle went without the appendix and was rebuilt. Merge the returned bundle by diff
  against what you sent, and build on the returned text (`references/process.md`).
- **When the author says finalize, deliver first.** Audits that are still running are reported in one line.

## 10. Check before anything is posted

In this order (`references/proofread.md`):

1. Build the paper copies; read the build line: 0 LaTeX errors, 0 undefined references, page count, size.
2. `resolve_lines.py` with zero unresolved passages; `check_numbers.py` and `check_replies.py` with zero faults;
   `rev_pdf_check.py` and `rev_passages.py` clean.
3. **The final proofread loop**: snapshot the PDF, its text with line numbers, the replies, the reviews and the
   submitted text into a fresh folder; one fresh agent per round with `assets/briefs/proofread_brief.md`; fix,
   rebuild, new agent, until it finds zero discrepancies. On the author's polished draft, one focused audit per round
   with the no-rebuild brief (see `references/process.md`). Round 1 found a citation on the wrong sentence, a "full
   ranking" promised and absent, a count that did not add up and one target with two names. The audit scope includes
   the unchanged captions and sentences next to every changed passage.
4. **A separate voice pass.** Three proofread rounds missed a sentence comparing each method "with the other N
   minus 1" and a slogan sentence; the author caught both. The proofread brief now checks for slogans (check E).
   Read the final text yourself, as the reviewer.
5. Look at the preview (`preview_replies.py`) and at every changed page of the paper rendered.
6. On the platform: the comment counts, that colors render, the case of reviewer IDs, the readers of each comment.
7. Say which checks cover this exact build and which an earlier one. Never call a gate passed while it runs.

## 11. Journal revisions

A journal revision is a project of weeks with new experiments, reruns and code changes; a conference rebuttal is
days of text. `references/journal-revision.md` has the comparison, the procedure and the patterns; the templates are
in `assets/journal/`.

- **Separate documents, one per job**: `01_SUMMARY.md`, `02_CHECKLIST.md`, `03_ACTION_PLAN.md`,
  `04_RESPONSE_LETTER.md`. "make separated docs obviously for each I told you" followed one combined 35 KB plan.
- **One ID per reviewer point from the reviewer's own numbering** (`R1.1`, `R1.m1` minor, `R1.c1` code and data),
  used in every document and branch. A scrambled first plan answered reviewer 2's comment as reviewer 1's.
- **Class each point before drafting**: new experiment, new analysis, text change, reply only. Then priority, owner
  and status. Decide scope in the plan; the letter follows the plan.
- **A point is never marked done before its result exists.** Status words are literal: not started, running,
  result in hand, written, blocked (why), partially done (n of m), superseded (why), declined (why); a half-run
  fan-out is never rounded up and a dropped item is never deleted. One package marked every point "Completed" while most experiments had not
  started. A job that exits 0 with missing rows has not succeeded; read the coverage file.
- **Every promise in the letter is checked against what was delivered** before it goes out (`check_letter.py`), and
  every default and number against the code and the run records: a draft stated defaults that contradicted the
  software's own table and the commands that ran.
- **The five-part block per comment**: the comment verbatim in full, Response, Revisions implemented, Location in
  revised manuscript (page and line of the tracked-changes copy, figure numbers), the checklist evidence.
- **Disagree in three moves** (what is valid, the method and cost reasons with citations, the text changed and the
  idea as an optional extension); **decline an experiment by answering its intent** with a controlled substitute;
  **for a comparator that could not be run**, say you tried, name the obstacle, state it in the manuscript.
- **The revision is a release**: one fix branch per bug the reruns expose, paper-only switches on their own branch,
  every rerun in a new timestamped folder with a manifest and coverage files (see `research-code`,
  `project-structure`, `paper-code-parity`).

```bash
python3 $S/check_letter.py 04_RESPONSE_LETTER.md --checklist 02_CHECKLIST.md --internal "[owner names],lock"
python3 $S/check_numbers.py --claims claims.json --trace 04_RESPONSE_LETTER.md --paper revised.txt
```

- `references/reply-structure.md`: the general response and per-reviewer templates, one spine for all replies, a summary for expert co-authors, tone, voice, the journal response.
- `references/platforms.md`: limits to verify, Markdown and math, counting, posting order, fields, previews, Word files for co-authors, base rates, journals.
- `references/chair-note.md`: the note to the area chair or editor, its order, the red and green device, phrases.
