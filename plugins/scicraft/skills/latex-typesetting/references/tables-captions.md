# Captions, tables and numbers

Read this before writing any caption or results table.

## 1. Caption anatomy

**A caption is a bold claim sentence, then the panel key with bold letters, then one source line.**

```latex
\caption[Overview]{\textbf{[Claim sentence: what the reader should see.]}
  \textbf{(a)}~[What panel a shows.] \textbf{(b)}~[What panel b shows; the dashed line marks chance.]
  Adapted from Fig.~2 of \citet{key}.}
```

- The optional argument is the list-of-figures entry: short, no claim.
- The claim sentence states what the reader should see, in the author's words where they exist
  (for example, one sentence that names the finding in plain words).
- **The bold title states the finding in plain words, with no count and no hype.** "All [N]
  ablations lower the combined score, [M] of them significantly" was struck as "absolute AI slop",
  and its count invites a question about the ones left over. The title that held stated the
  finding in plain words without a count. Counts and tests go into the caption body or the text,
  in full.
- **Every marker in the figure is named in the caption**: a dashed line, a color, a shaded band,
  an asterisk. A final proofread caught "a caption that did not name a dashed line".
- Caption package settings in all profiles:
  `font=small,labelfont=bf,labelsep=colon,justification=justified,singlelinecheck=false`; tables
  `position=top` with 6 pt skip; figures 5 to 7 pt skip.
- Keep the last word of the bold title from standing alone: `\mbox{last word}` or a `~`.

### Source line

| The figure | Source line |
|---|---|
| changed from a published figure | "Adapted from Fig.~2b of \citet{key}." |
| a whole published figure, unchanged | "Taken from Fig.~2 of \citet{key}." |
| the author's own unpublished work | in words, no figure number, no citation: "Adapted from a manuscript under revision at [journal]." |
| drawn for this document | no source line |

- Never write "Reproduced". Never mention slides, decks, posters, working figures or licenses in
  a caption: "No one knows about that". People who generated data go to a footnote or a
  disclosures page, not into the caption.
- Unpublished own work is never cited as evidence; see `bibliography.md`, section 4.

### Table caption contents

A table caption states: what a row and a column are, the number of runs or seeds and what the
spread is (SD or CI), the color key, **what an asterisk means** ("* adjusted p < .05, Benjamini-Hochberg"), what bold and
underline mean when they are used, what every other symbol means,
and what the last columns are. The reader should never have to guess an encoding.

When the table uses rank fills, set the words in the caption on the same fills, so the caption is
the legend: "Fills mark the \rankkey{rankfirst}{first}, \rankkey{ranksecond}{second} and
\rankkey{rankthird}{third} rank per column" (`\rankkey` is in `assets/venue/housestyle.tex`). The
author asked for this on a benchmark table: "when you say first, second, third, please color the
first, second, third also".

## 2. Results tables are generated, never typed

- **Write the table body from the record (JSON, CSV) with a script; the script may change layout,
  never a value.** Every generated body starts
  `% generated by <script> from <record>; do not edit`. Regenerate after every change to the
  record; captions stay in the manuscript around `\input{tab_x}`.
- `scripts/make_table.py record.json --rank-columns 2-8 --rank-low 9 -o tab_x.tex` writes such a
  body: booktabs rules, group rows, rank fills with superscript ranks, ± SD in tiny gray,
  `adjustbox` max width. One US-spelling normalizer was applied in the generator in the original
  project; any normalization lives there, never in hand edits.
- A poster or a talk takes the manuscript's tables unchanged through `\input`, with a wrapper that
  neutralizes `table`, `\caption` and `\label`.

## 3. The table recipe

From a benchmark table in a submitted ML paper (values below are placeholders):

```latex
\begingroup\scriptsize\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2.35pt}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.0}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\linewidth}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{\hspace{7pt}}c@{}}
\toprule
 & [Task A] & [Task B] & [Task C] & mean \\
\addlinespace[-1pt]
 & \hnote{[n] sets} & \hnote{[n] sets} & \hnote{[n] sets} & rank \hnote{$\downarrow$} \\
\midrule
\grp{5}{trained from scratch}
{[Baseline 1]} & .610\pmsd{.04} & \cellcolor{rankthird}.480\textsuperscript{3}\pmsd{.06} & ... \\
\grp{5}{pretrained}
\method{} & \cellcolor{rankfirst}\textbf{.660}\textsuperscript{1}\pmsd{.04} & ... \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{adjustbox}
\endgroup
```

- **booktabs only**: `\toprule`, `\midrule`, `\cmidrule(lr){a-b}` under grouped headers,
  `\bottomrule`. No vertical rules.
- **Group rows instead of a category column**: `\grp{ncols}{label}` sets an italic label across
  the width. A category repeated down every row wastes a column.
- **In a benchmark table, group comparators by what they were trained on, and name each one's
  input unit in a column.** Groups such as "trained from scratch", "pretrained on [corpus]",
  "foundation model, zero-shot", "fitted on the test data", "no model"; a unit column such as
  token, image patch, graph, or none (in a biology benchmark: cell, cell with neighbors). The
  reader sees why two rows differ without the text. Rotated side labels without a column name
  were tried first, overlapped, and were replaced by this form.
- Two-line headers joined with `\addlinespace[-1pt]`; a small gray line under a header for sample
  counts (`\hnote{[n] samples}`); direction arrows in the header ("mean rank ↓", "Elo ↑").
- **Rank fills** for first, second and third per column: `D3ECE3`, `D8EBFF`, `FDEDCE`, each with
  a superscript rank so the order survives gray-scale printing.
- Error after the value, tiny and gray: `.58{\tiny\color{black!62}$\pm$.09}` (`\pmsd{.09}`).
- **An asterisk only for a significant result, as the caption defines it.** A revision round removed
  the mark from two cells whose adjusted p missed .05. Best and second-best by value use `\textbf` and
  `\underline` only when the caption declares that pair.
- Ablation deltas shaded by size and direction: `\cellcolor{warn!N}` (RGB 210,85,75, a fall) or
  `\cellcolor{ok!N}` (RGB 65,145,85, a rise), N proportional to the shift over the column maximum
  and at most 60. Color encodes size and direction; an asterisk encodes significance; the
  caption says so.
- `\scriptsize`, `\tabcolsep` 2.35 pt, `\arraystretch` 1.0 for a dense benchmark table; set
  inside `\begingroup ... \endgroup` so the rest of the document keeps its spacing.
- **Shrink a wide table with `adjustbox` `max width=\linewidth`; never with `\resizebox`**, which
  also enlarges a narrow table until its text is larger than the body.
- Narrow text columns ragged right: `\newcolumntype{R}[1]{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{#1}}`.
  "Below about 40 characters, ragged wins."
- A missing value is `\na` (a gray dash), never a blank or zero.
- Comparator citations go inside the table cell when the table travels (poster, slides).
- A cell whose text starts with `[` right after `\\` is read as the optional argument of `\\`
  ("Missing number"): brace it, `{[Baseline 1]}`. `make_table.py` does this.

## 4. Numbers

- Thousands with a thin group: `$50{,}000$`; powers `$10^{6}$`.
- Unit after a thin space, upright: `5\,\textmu m` or the `\um` macro. A final proofread caught
  an italic unit.
- Bounded scores and p values without the leading zero in tables: `.58`, `$-$.017`,
  `adjusted $p=.002$`, `$p<.001$`. True minus: `$-$`.
- `\pv{.002}` prints `p = .002` with italic p and thin spaces.
- **A reporting threshold applies to every result alike and is stated in the document.** Print
  the exact p value of every reported test or state the one rule ("p values below .001 are given
  as <.001"); never drop the inconvenient ones.
- Defaults are stated once in one sentence with a pointer to a defaults table; a setting that
  differs from the default is reported where its result is reported.
- Derive every number from the primary record (per-task values), never from a summary sentence;
  keep an errata list when two places in the paper disagree.
